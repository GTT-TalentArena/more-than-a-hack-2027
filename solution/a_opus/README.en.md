```bash
python -m solution.a_opus --out evaluation/results/a_opus.csv
python evaluation/validate_submission.py evaluation/results/a_opus.csv
```

- It runs **from the repository root**, and `-m` takes the dotted module name (`solution.a_opus`), not a path.
- It reads the data from `MTAH_DATA_DIR` (or `./data`) through `games.asset_paths()`; `--data-dir` changes it. If
  it can't find them, it says where it looked and exits with code 2.
- It writes 199,319 `frame,label` rows, with `X` outside the two scored halves and `H`/`A`/`D` inside.
- It takes **about 10 s** on a laptop without a GPU (the synthetic ATD, the same size as the real one, is read in

- It is **deterministic**: two runs give the same file byte for byte (checked with SHA-256), with `\n` line breaks
  on any operating system.
- Everything it prints is **aggregate** (counts, percentages, durations, fitted constants). No rows, no frames. It's
  the log that can be returned to the LLM under agreement A7.

## Approach

A possession is an interval, and `spellF1` scores intervals. That's why the candidate doesn't decide frame by
frame: it computes **per-frame evidence** and then looks for **the globally optimal H/A/D sequence** with a cost for
each change of state.

```
maximise   Σ_t evidence(state_t, t)  −  λ · (number of changes)      with D ≥ 2 s
```

It's a three-state HMM/Viterbi (`decode.py`) and it solves exactly what the study recommended (section 4.5): a
single knob (λ) for the flicker vs short-possession dilemma, boundaries where the evidence changes and no systematic
delay, because it's processed offline with past and future. The minimum stoppage duration is imposed exactly with a
cumulative sum, so the recursion is O(1) per frame and a half is decoded in under a second. The
`test_decoder_is_exact` test compares the solution with brute force on 300 random problems.

### Team evidence (H vs A)

- **Control zone.** For each team, the distance from the ball to its nearest player goes into a kernel
  `K(d) = exp(−½·(max(d − d0, 0)/σ)²)`, multiplied by a ball–player relative-speed gate. A ball passing quickly
  next to an opponent isn't controlled. The frame's evidence is `α·(C_H − C_A)`: in a duel, with both teams close,
  it cancels out.
- **No ball isn't a dead ball.** It's the baseline's main error: when the ball isn't visible, the evidence is zero,
  not `D`, and the decoder keeps the state. Gaps of up to 2 s are filled with a straight line between the two
  observations, with a weight that decays with the gap's length.
- **"A loose ball stays with the last team".** In flights and occlusions the evidence is exactly zero and the
  decoder ties. Ties are resolved on purpose: the change of team is placed **as late as possible**, at the new
  team's first touch.
- **Touches without control.** Changing possession costs λ = 5 nats and a flicker inside a possession costs twice
  that. With α = 0.4, it takes ~10 frames of clear control to change and ~20 for a flicker, so a deflection or a
  clearance without control doesn't change possession, as `CHALLENGE.md` asks. (λ = 4 in v1.1 and λ = 3 from v1.3;
  see the sections on real runs below.)
- **Touches (v1.3; detector by velocity change from v1.4).** A peak in the ball's net velocity change, with one
  player clearly the nearest, gives 1.5 nats to their team. A touch alone doesn't change possession, but it dates
  the change at the right frame when control follows.
- **Smart Tagging as a weak prior.** Phases with a team (`BUILD UP`, `PROGRESSION`, `DEFENDING IN …`) add a small
  nudge: at most 0.03 nats per frame, less than a tenth of a frame of control. It only weighs when there is no ball.
  The tagger emits phases in pairs over the same interval (one team's `BUILD UP` and the other's `DEFENDING IN
  ATTACKING THIRD`, etc.), so each interval counts only once.
- **Ball-free model.** It's a logistic regression on the shape and movement of the teams: relative position, width,
  speed towards the opponent's goal and block, oriented by each half's direction of attack. It only acts where there
  is no ball (41% of the scored frames).

### Dead-ball evidence (D vs alive)

It's kept separate from the team evidence, on purpose. Who is close to the ball says which team has it **if it's
alive**, but almost nothing about whether it is: the player about to take a throw-in is as close to the ball as a
dribbler. That's why team evidence never counts against `D`.

| term | what it captures | magnitude |
|---|---|---|
| out of bounds | ball seen more than 0.5 m outside a line; the crossing is dated with the straight line interpolated from the last observation inside | +1 nat per observation, +0.5 per interpolated frame outside; ×0.3 if it comes back in within 1 s (projected aerial ball) |
| exit | last observation next to a line and leaving, followed by a gap ≥ 1.5 s | +3 nats in total |
| still ball | ball stopped ≥ 1 s (placed ball); ×3 at a corner or the centre of the pitch, ×2 in the six-yard box or at the penalty spot, ×0.3 if it's stuck to a player (goalkeeper with the ball in their hands) | +0.06 nats per frame |
| kick after a still ball | the ball leaves at more than 5 m/s after being still (searched from 1 s before the end of the still run, because the slow fit anticipates it) | −0.5 nats per frame, 10 frames |
| restart after a long gap (v1.3) | the ball reappears slow (in hands or placed) and someone touches it within 5 s | +0.05 per frame until the touch; −0.5 per frame for the next 10 |
| fast ball | ball at more than 6 m/s inside the pitch | −0.08 nats per frame |
| collective speed | mixture of two Gaussians on the players' mean speed (stopped = slow component). It can't vote `D` where the ball is seen moving inside the pitch in ≥ 20% of the ±1 s | LLR clipped to ±3, /25 per frame |
| long absence of the ball (v1.2) | each frame of a ball gap ≥ 2 s. The intensity is the odds ratio between the fraction of long-gap frames that a first decoding (which doesn't look at visibility) puts in `D` and the overall fraction; short gaps don't count | 0.5 × log-odds-ratio / 25 per frame, clipped to ±3 |
| kick-off formation | almost everyone in their own half, slow, ball (if visible) at the centre | +0.15 nats per frame |
| Smart Tagging `SET PIECES` | the restart is inside the window; only documented for kick-offs (~3.5 s after it opens) | +0.06 from 2 s before to 3 s after it opens; −0.06 for the second after it closes |

Stoppages start **as early as possible** (as soon as the ball goes out, what follows isn't play) and end **as late
as possible** (the restart is when the ball is struck). Minimum duration: 2 s.

### Label-free self-calibration

Some constants are estimated from the match itself, without GT. They are ATD statistics, not fits to a score:

- **Control radius**: `d0` and `σ` are scaled with the median distance to the nearest player for slow balls.
- **Still-ball threshold**: the ball's position noise is measured with second differences, and the threshold rises
  if that noise requires it.
- **Speed model**: the mixture of two Gaussians is fitted with EM on the match.
- **Smart Tagging**: the polarity and reliability of each code are measured by their agreement with the team that
  clearly has the ball. A code that disagrees is inverted and one at 50% is ignored.
- **Ball-free model**: it's trained on the frames where proximity clearly names a team (self-supervision), with
  cross-validation by half. Each half is predicted with the other half's model, and the accuracy on that unseen data
  sets its weight (0 if it doesn't exceed 55%, full weight from 75%).

## Validation on synthetic data

`synth.py` simulates 22 agents with dribbles, passes, interceptions, tackles, loose balls, shots, balls out over the
touchline and goal line, fouls and every restart, including kick-offs. On top of that it adds the noise the real
ATD documents:

- ball missing ~50% of the time, in bursts, more in the air, in crowds and with the ball stopped;
- position noise and wild detections;
- aerial balls projected far from the real point;
- fragmented tracks that jump between the 46 slots;
- a Smart Tagging that marks only some restarts and phases, with shifted edges.

Its parameters are invented and **different from the candidate's**. It's for checking that the pipeline is correct
and for seeing the shape of the curves, not for predicting the real score.

Results on three full synthetic matches (seeds 0, 1 and 2), with the default configuration:

What the synthetic data shows:

- **Boundaries land well.** The detected H↔A changes have a median offset of 0 frames (p90 of 9 frames with the
  ball visible and 16 without it). The ends of stoppages land within ±1 frame.
- **What gets lost is short possessions.** Almost none under 2 s is detected, and half of those of 2–5 s. Below
  ~1 s the available evidence isn't enough even for a well-calibrated Bayesian decoder, because the change prior
  weighs more.
- **Stoppages for fouls start late** (~3 s): there's no whistle in the tracking, only the collective slowdown.
- **Sensitivity to λ** (v1.1, mean of the three seeds): 3 → 0.761 · 4 → 0.753 · 5 → 0.731. The synthetic data
  prefers a low λ. 4 is used rather than 3 because real noise (untracked players in duels, false detections, aerial
  projections) produces more spurious evidence than the synthetic data. It's an a priori compromise, not a fit.
- **Ablations** (v1.1, mean of the three seeds, from 0.753): without the speed model, 0.668 (−0.08); without the
  ball-free model, 0.717 (−0.04); without Smart Tagging, 0.735 (−0.02); without the speed filter by ball activity,
  0.753 (in the synthetic data play is never slow: the filter is meant for the real data). On synthetic data the
  ball-free model is right 97% of the time because the simulation ties team shape very closely to possession.

## First real run (v1.0) and the v1.1 changes

Run by Jimy locally on 21 Oct 2026. Only the aggregate log went back to the LLM (A7); it isn't a public evaluation.

**What it confirmed:**
- The whole ATD is read: 199,319 contiguous records, line structure read from the XML.
- Team assignment is correct: Smart Tagging comes out at H 314 / A 288 / N/A 36, exactly what DATA.md says.
- H is Barça: 65% of live possession. The direction of attack is clear in both halves.
- The ball-free model is right **82–85%** of the time on unseen data: on the real data it's a useful source.
- All Smart Tagging codes have the expected polarity. `BUILD UP` and `PROGRESSION` agree 87–89% with who has the
  ball; the final-third and box ones, 60–71%.

**What the synthetic data didn't have:**
- The ATD ball is **smoothed** (position noise ~0) and **has no short gaps**: the ≤ 2 s interpolation fills
  nothing. When the ball is missing, it's missing for more than 2 s in a row.
- The ball's third channel is speed, not height.
- The `SET PIECES` windows are much longer than assumed: the total evidence implies ~15–25 s on average.

**What went wrong:**
- Only **48 direct H↔A changes** in the whole match (162 possessions, 115 stoppages, 54.7% `D`). Almost every change
  went through a stoppage. Two code bugs partly explain it:
  - the kick after a still ball was never detected (the slow fit anticipates it and the search didn't reach it);
  - with long windows, the `SET PIECES` evidence spilled several seconds into the play after the restart.

**v1.1 changes:**
- The two bugs fixed.
- Paired Smart Tagging phases count only once.
- Collective speed no longer votes `D` when the ball is seen in play.
- λ = 4.
- The log adds label-free checks: where the restart falls inside the windows, what the ball does in frames decoded
  as `D` and as alive, stretches of clear control by the "ball-free" team, and the curve of possessions and changes
  vs λ.

**About the 54.7% `D`:** it isn't necessarily an error. Three public hints point to a high `D` on the full match:
- `starter/make_dummy_submission.py` says the dead ball is "the most common label". In the public window it's the
  least common, so it's talking about the full match.
- The macroF1 of `always_home` published in `run_evaluation.py` (0.1627) doesn't match the public window (it would
  give 0.210). With the full GT it implies that H takes up ~32% of the scored frames. With Barça at ~60–70%
  possession, that leaves `D` at ~45–54%.
- LaLiga's effective playing time is around 55 min out of ~106.

The public window (19.6% `D`) is the start of the match and isn't representative.

## Second real run (v1.1) and the v1.2 changes

**Reproducibility.** v1.1, rerun, gives exactly the same file (SHA-256 `a669fb61…`), which is criterion C1.

**What the label-free checks said** (run 2):
- 184 possessions, 108 stoppages (47.2% `D`, now within the expected range) and 76 direct H↔A changes.
- **The ball is missing mostly when the ball is dead:** it's visible in 91% of the frames decoded as alive and in 24%
  of those decoded as `D`. It's question L4 of the study, and the answer is yes: a long absence is evidence of a
  stoppage (study 3.3).
- The `SET PIECES` windows last ~35 s, and the decoded restart falls at a median of 7 s after they open.
- There are no long stretches of ignored opponent control, and the λ curve has no plateau.

**What the public window said** (evaluations no. 1 and 2, recorded by Jimy; only used to understand the type of
error):
- The alive/dead decision is good (alive/dead F1 0.96) and the team is right 87% of the time.
- **The boundaries land badly:** only 3 of the 20 real transitions have one of ours within ±1 s.

**v1.2 changes, all label-free and taken from the study:**
- **Long absence of the ball as stoppage evidence** (study 3.3, point 3, and L4). There are two passes: the first
  doesn't look at visibility, and its association between long gaps and `D` (a hard EM step) gives the intensity
  for the second. Short gaps don't count.
  - First I tried giving evidence to each gap by its length: on synthetic data it created false stoppages from
    bursts of short gaps.
  - Then a visibility Markov chain: it turned the synthetic data's frame-by-frame flicker into huge evidence.
  - Both were discarded.
- **Ball-free model without lag** (study 3.3, point 4, and P6). A team's shape responds to possession with a delay
  of seconds, so 2 s means shifted 1–2 s into the future and 1 s into the past are added, and the regression learns
  the offset. The log prints the accuracy profile against the shift: it must peak at 0.
- **Change dated at the first touch** (study 3.2, 4.6, P5). Each H↔A change moves back, by up to 1 s, through the
  frames in which the new team is within reach of the ball and at least as close as the old one. A flight stops it:
  a pass still belongs to the receiver.
- **Ball lag relative to the players.** It's measured with carried balls: the shift δ that best matches the ball's
  speed to its carrier's. If it's clear (≥ 2 frames and ≥ 5% better than not shifting), it's corrected. I first used
  positions and discarded it, because it mistakes for a lag the 0.5 m the ball runs ahead of the player in a dribble.

On synthetic data these changes are neutral (0.757 vs 0.753), which is expected: they calibrate themselves, and the
synthetic data has no lag, no long gaps tied to `D` and no long duels.

## Third real run (v1.2) and the v1.3 changes

**The v1.2 log confirmed four things:**
- There's no ball lag (flat speed profile; correction 0).
- The ball-free model improves to 87.9 / 83.8%, with the accuracy peak at offset 0.
- Gaps ≥ 2 s cover 40.6% of frames, and 87.7% of them fall in `D` (+0.042 nats/frame).
- Dating at the first touch barely acts (5 of 71 changes).

**The public window** (evaluation no. 3) gives spellF1 0.4800, the same as v1.1, with a bootstrap interval of
0.29–0.67. 4 of 17 possessions are missing, and the transitions that match arrive late (+9 frames median).

**v1.3 changes:**
- **Touches** (study 3.1 and P3). A touch is a peak in the ball's acceleration (local quadratic fit over ±4 frames)
  above the 95th percentile of balls in free flight (self-calibrated), with one player clearly closer than any
  opponent. It's worth 1.5 nats for their team, less than λ: a touch alone (a deflection or a block) doesn't change
  possession, but it dates the change when control follows. It's also the only trace a one-touch interception
  leaves.
- **λ = 3.** With touches, the evidence for a change is sharper and better placed; all the synthetic sweeps prefer
  3; and the real match is still under-segmented (185 possessions, 71 direct changes). A flicker inside a possession
  still requires 6 nats: four opponent touches, or one touch and ~11 frames of control.
- **Restart after a long gap** (study 3.4). If the ball reappears slow (in hands or placed) and someone touches it
  within 5 s, the stoppage extends to that touch and ends there. It's the typical throw-in case, which with the ball
  in hands isn't "still" and previously had no anchor.

Synthetic: touches alone with λ 4 → 0.767; λ 3 without touches → 0.768; **both together → 0.782** (v1.2: 0.757).
The restart after a long gap has no effect on the synthetic data, which barely has long gaps in its stoppages.

## Fourth real run (v1.3) and the v1.4 changes

**What the v1.3 log showed:** λ = 3 did act (197 possessions, 83 direct changes), but the acceleration-based touch
detector **only found 70 in the whole match**, against the ~1,500 it finds on synthetic data. With so few touches,
the restart after a gap only fired twice. The likely cause is the ball smoothing in the real ATD (median of the
second difference ~0): it flattens the acceleration peaks, while projected aerial balls inflate the free-flight
threshold (24.9 m/s²).

**The public window** (evaluation no. 4) gave exactly the same as v1.2 (0.4800): the v1.3 changes didn't act in
those 5 minutes.

**v1.4 change: touch detector by net velocity change**, |v(t, t+6) − v(t−6, t)|. What it measures doesn't depend
on how sharp the kick looks after smoothing: a pass or a reception changes the speed by several m/s with any
smoothing.
- **Threshold:** the 95th percentile of free-flight frames, with nobody within 4 m for the whole window, so that a
  nearby kick doesn't contaminate it. Minimum 3 m/s.
- **Assignment:** each team's minimum distance within ±3 frames around the peak, with a 2 m radius and a 0.75 m
  margin.
- **Log:** it counts peaks, peaks with a player close, assigned and ambiguous, to see in which filter they're lost.

To test it, the generator now has `--ball-style real`: it fills gaps under 2 s and smooths the ball, like the real
ATD. Results on synthetic data:
- **Real style:** v1.4 0.785 vs 0.775 without touches.
- **Noisy:** 0.775 vs 0.768 without touches (the acceleration-based detector gave 0.782).
- **Restart after a gap:** −0.003 on synthetic data, within the noise. It's kept because in the real data long gaps
  are stoppages (87%) and it's the case it anchors.

## Fifth real run (v1.4) and the v1.5 changes

**What the v1.4 log showed:** 422 touch peaks, 261 with a player close and 213 assigned. More than in v1.3, but far
from what you'd expect. In free flight, the ball's velocity change has p50/p90/p95 of 0.90/3.73/5.29 m/s: the ATD
smooths the ball at a ~1 s scale.

It was reproduced with `synth.py --ball-style real --smooth-half 12`, which gives almost the same signature
(0.48/3.76/5.21 m/s, 884 peaks). From here on, that's the main test bench.

**v1.5 changes:**
- **Touches at the match's scale.** The detector tries half-windows of ±6, ±12 and ±25 frames and keeps, without
  labels, the one that separates the most peaks with a player close. With strong smoothing it chooses ±25 (1,128
  touches vs 449 at ±6); with light smoothing or noise it stays at ±6. The log prints the profile.
- **Ball-free model also with the ball in view but no clear control** (flights, duels, a smoothed ball between
  players), at half its weight. Before, it only acted without the ball, which in live play is 7% of the time. It's at
  half and not full weight (on synthetic data full weight gives +0.014 vs +0.010) because the real model is right
  86% of the time, not 97%: at half weight it takes more than 6 s in a row of confident error to invent a flicker.

**Tried and switched off** (no improvement on smoothed synthetic data, −0.003; they remain behind switches):
- dating changes by reach without the speed gate;
- dating restarts at the ball speed's half-rise.

**Tried and discarded:** λ 2–2.5, a touch at 2.5 nats and an out-of-bounds margin of 0.2 m. All within the noise.

## Sixth real run (v1.5) and the v1.6 change

**The v1.5 log is healthy:** 214 possessions (median 10.4 s), 113 stoppages (49.3% `D`), 101 direct H↔A changes and
no long stretch of ignored opponent control. The ball-free model on frames without clear control has moved
possessions and changes towards what's expected.

**Touches are still weak:** at the chosen scale (±25) the threshold came out at 12 m/s and only 315 were assigned. At
that scale, "free flight" (nobody within 4 m for 2 s) is almost always long aerial balls, whose curved projection
inflates the reference (p90 9.5 m/s).

**v1.6 change:** the threshold's reference is a **ground ball** in free flight (both chords below 12 m/s). It
measures the tracker's own noise: high on noisy synthetic data (threshold 6.4 m/s), almost zero on a smoothed ball
(3.4–4.2 m/s). The synthetic data doesn't have that aerial bias, so there the change is neutral (0.784 / 0.786, the
same as v1.5). It fixes a failure measured on the real data.

## Known limitations

- **Possessions under ~2 s.** `EVALUATION.md` suggests the GT has quite a few (section 2.5 of the study). This
  candidate barely sees them. It's the biggest margin for improvement, together with stronger control evidence (for
  example, touches detected by a change in the ball's direction).
- **Start of the stoppage on fouls and offsides**: with no whistle, it's dated by the collective slowdown and
  arrives late.
- **Aerial ball**: the ATD projects it onto the ground from the camera. The speed gate and the re-entry rule limit
  the damage, but don't eliminate it.
- **A priori parameters**: λ, α and the D weights come from reasoning and from synthetic data, not from the real
  match. The log's label-free checks, not the public window, are what should guide changes.
- **Few changes in play**: v1.0 decoded very few direct H↔A changes on the real data. v1.1 fixes two bugs and lowers
  λ, but it still needs checking with the λ curve and the log's stretches of ignored control.

## P3 and P4 work after the delivery (#15, #16)

Neither the model nor its CSV change: Viterbi is still the default decoder, and the output is byte-identical. What
was added is for checking and integrating.

**Alternative decoders (#16).** With `--set decode.method=hysteresis` or `--set decode.method=vote`, the same evidence
is decoded in two more ways:
- **hysteresis:** CUSUM with backdating and the same costs as Viterbi (study 4.4).
- **vote:** majority over a ±1 s window, like the baseline but on this evidence.

`python -m solution.a_opus.compare_decoders <synthetic matches>` compares them. On 5 synthetic matches (3 noisy and 2
with the ball smoothed like the real ATD):

| decoder | spellF1 | possessions / real | fragmentation | real short ones (< 5 s) kept | false ones under 2 s | transition P / R at ±1 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Viterbi** | **0.783** | 1,580 / 1,915 | 0.88 | 214 / 627 | **17** | 0.72 / 0.63 |
| hysteresis | 0.761 | 2,010 / 1,915 | 1.11 | 298 / 627 | 195 | 0.62 / 0.69 |
| voting | 0.659 | 2,560 / 1,915 | 1.42 | 267 / 627 | 722 | 0.50 / 0.70 |

- **Viterbi** removes noise without imposing a minimum duration: it keeps a third of the real short possessions and
  barely invents false ones.
- **Hysteresis** confirms a change as soon as the accumulated excess exceeds the cost, and dates it where the excess
  started. It recovers more short possessions, but invents 11 times more false ones.
- **Voting** fragments like the baseline.

**Explicit cases (#15, #16).** In `python -m solution.a_opus.tests`:
- `test_p3_control_cases`: a 15 m/s pass grazing a defender isn't control, a reception is, and a duel gives no net
  evidence.
- `test_p3_p4_scenarios`, with the candidate's costs:
  - an interception changes possession at the new team's first contact;
  - a rebound or deflection, a duel without subsequent control and isolated noise don't change it;
  - a real 1.2 s recovery is kept;
  - a change across a ball gap is dated where the new team's evidence starts.
- `test_p4_first_frames_of_a_half`: a half that starts stopped decodes `D` first; one that starts in play takes the
  team of its first evidence from frame 0.

**Per-frame evidence (#15, P0 contract §6).** `--evidence salidas/evidencia.npz` writes, for the whole video:
- `control_H_score`, `control_A_score` and `control_valid`: control strength 0..1, not a probability;
- `nearest_H_m`, `nearest_A_m` and `distance_valid`;
- `touch_H` and `touch_A`;
- `team_llr` and `dead_llr` (nats), and the decoded state.

Without an observed or interpolated ball, `control_valid` is false and the scores are NaN. No absence is turned into
evidence. They are frame-by-frame match data: local only, never in the repository.

## P3: integration of P2 (#14)

`solution/a_opus/p2.py` runs Pablo's detector (`solution/dead_ball.py`) on the whole match, exactly as he validated
it: his reader, his thresholds and his events. Its candidates are turned into one more dead-ball evidence term:
- **`tagged_preparation`:** `D` from the first still-ball frame to the restart, and alive right after. It's what
  `setpiece_llr` doesn't know: at what point in the `SET PIECES` window the ball is played.
- **`initial_kickoff_context`:** `D` before the first kick-off of each half.
- **`observed_exit`:** weight 0, because `out` and `exit` already count exits.

It's off by default (`p2.enabled=0`): the delivered candidate A doesn't change. With P2 off, the CSV of a synthetic
match is byte-identical to `main`'s. The P2 variant is launched with:

```bash
python -m solution.a_opus.with_p2 --out salidas/a_p2.csv --log salidas/a_p2_log.txt
```

The log adds a line with P2's candidates by type, without frames. `python -m solution.a_opus.compare_p2 <synthetic
matches>` compares the two variants.

**Synthetic result** (5 matches with the ball smoothed like the real ATD; 3 of them at a ~1 s scale,
`--smooth-half 12`):

| variant | spellF1 | macroF1 | possessions / real | false ones under 2 s | restart error p50 / p90 (frames) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, without P2 | 0.7919 | 0.9310 | 1,650 / 1,984 | 18 | 7 / 75 |
| A + P2 as is | 0.7919 | 0.9310 | 1,650 / 1,984 | 18 | 7 / 75 |
| A + P2, rule with a 1 s margin (experiment) | 0.7907 | 0.9284 | 1,663 / 1,984 | 24 | 15 / 78 |
| same, restart dated at the movement (experiment) | 0.7915 | 0.9299 | 1,656 / 1,984 | 22 | 9 / 77 |

- **As is, P2 finds no preparations.** 68 of 82 windows have the ball still in the restart area, but none meets
  ≥ 2 m/s in the 3 frames immediately after. With the ball smoothed, the speed takes a while to rise: in 54 of them
  the movement arrives within 1 s. It only contributes the kick-off context, and there A already decodes `D`.
- **With a 1 s margin** (only as an experiment, without changing `dead_ball.py`) it does find them, but A doesn't
  improve: it already dates those restarts with `setpiece`, `static`, `kick` and `restart`, and P2's restart falls
  a few frames before the kick.

**Real run, log only** (`with_p2 --log`, without scoring the public window). It confirms the synthetic result:

| | v1.6 (without P2) | with P2 |
|---|---|---|
| P2 candidates | — | 0 `tagged_preparation`, 2 `initial_kickoff_context` (1 with a restart), 64 `observed_exit` (weight 0) |
| `p2` evidence (nats) | — | +137 / −5 |
| possessions (median) | 219 (10.4 s) | 216 (9.9 s) |
| stoppages (scored frames in `D`) | 113 (49.3%) | 111 (49.9%) |
| H↔A changes | 106 | 105 |

On the real ATD, the rule doesn't find any restart preparation either. Only the context before the kick-off acts:
2 fewer stoppages and +0.6 points of `D`. Without labels it's impossible to know whether that's right.

**Decision:** the integration stays available and switched off. For P2 to contribute in A, the preparation rule
would have to accept the smoothed ball (for example, sustained movement within 1 s after the still ball, dated where
it starts). That's a change in `dead_ball.py`, with its thresholds fixed in advance: it's proposed in #14.
